## Finite authority

The selected bindings are source `c191-experimental-0.1`, profile
`guided-box-contact-0.1`, backend `blender-bullet-c190-0.1`, and recorded output
`glb-guided-contact-0.1`. The explicitly invoked prototype supports only the
declared seven-part assembly and its admitted edits. It does not extend the
existing parser or introduce an Agent binding. Earlier source, Agent, GLB and
default execution behavior remain unchanged.

The source owns two 1 kg moving bodies and four shoe/stop contact boxes. Native
helpers, frames, initialization, feed motors and solver settings are declared.
Blender/Bullet owns carriage motion. The recorded projection must preserve every
native carriage X sample and timestamp and meet the independently frozen
0.2 mm mapping bound. Positive cases retain the 0.5 mm penetration limit,
25 ms settling gate and twenty unrelated-pair nonoverlap checks.

Ordinary defaults remain 100,000 operations and 30,000 ms. The qualification
inputs explicitly request 1,000,000 operations and 80,000 ms; this adoption
authorizes those finite ceilings for the identified C191 qualification attempts.
Source requests do not authorize themselves. The implementation must separately
enforce all source, native object, sample, byte, deadline and cancellation caps.

The runtime, repeat-build, saved-scene, native-causality, malformed-result,
rollback, independent delivery and receiving-application gates remain mandatory.
Every failure must remain visible. A later semantic correction requires a
separate reviewed amendment with prior inputs and outcomes preserved.

The experimental implementation and its integration are distinct from a
production backend, general collision dynamics, a complete feature release,
comparative authoring benefit and completion of the full project goal.
