## The product question

The adopted [impulse contract](../../language-design/OK-LANG-004-source-impulses-and-free-sphere-dynamics.md) and [center-of-mass force contract](../../language-design/OK-LANG-005-finite-center-of-mass-force-intervals.md) define the existing free homogeneous sphere scope, including angular response to instantaneous impulses. A finite applied torque could express a motor-like spin-up or braking interval with continuously changing angular velocity. That is a distinct physical input from one instantaneous impulse or an authored visual rotation. It should be useful for inspecting and repairing the timing and strength of a rotating carrier while keeping source, numerical state and recorded delivery aligned.

Use a visibly asymmetric massless marker assembly, or `no_body` visual descendants, carried by a free homogeneous spherical body as the representative task. Only the carrier's own spherical body supplies mass and inertia; the visual assembly is not a compound physical body. Two independent occurrences share a pinned source definition. An Agent changes the torque magnitude or interval on one occurrence, checks the changed angular state and recorded orientation, and leaves its sibling, geometry, materials, images and unrelated translational motion unchanged. Include acceleration, braking through zero angular velocity, an invalid edit, and a successful repair.

This task would remain a free-sphere rotational experiment. It would not establish a wheel-ground contact model, a joint, a constrained motor, suspension, general rigid-body inertia, friction, fluid drag, a locomotion controller, or live engine physics. ## Settle the smallest coherent physical contract

Review a **fixed-axis collinear scope first**. For a homogeneous sphere, the scalar inertia is `I = 2 m r^2 / 5`. Collinear torque and spin admit an independent piecewise analytic reference: angular acceleration is `tau / I`, angular velocity is affine over each constant-torque interval, and signed angle is quadratic. Overlapping supported intervals can be summed and split at exact source event boundaries. This offers a bounded first increment without treating a general changing-axis rotation as a scalar angle.

The contract review must decide whether that scope is useful. If a general world-vector torque is required, write a separately justified orientation-integration and numerical-error contract before freezing inputs. A quaternion cannot in general be obtained by exponentiating the integral of an arbitrarily changing angular-velocity vector. Do not silently use the collinear analytic formula outside its adopted domain or select a tolerance after observing candidate output.

Define zero spin, zero torque, opposing torque, reversal through zero and finite numeric bounds.
