# C065 independent cost and retained-evidence review

## Decision and scope

**NO-GO for the selected detached four-product bridge with one charged materialization/admission per copied scalar.** Its four producer copies consume the largest possible four-operation arithmetic reduction before compilation, publication, retrieval, consumer copies, extra authority checks or fallback. This is a finite rejection of that concrete schedule. It is not a lower-bound theorem for every expression graph, zero-copy reference, native-integer representation or coefficient-cache extension.

Baseline is commit `a7452d0063b785c1bfb02c45844ef341e3e0bf0b`, tree `f063d6c54b0bbca286353169f6d7505cfaa8ccb8`. This review read the pinned source, the C064 records and retained C060/C061 evidence. It imported no OntoKin module, ran no product request or test, continued no failed tail and changed no runtime source. A successful source-design review would authorize only its stated development increment; completing either original request below the unchanged cap would require separate evidence.

## Real opportunity and existing work

The head in original P11 rotates about Y. Its left/right eye vectors have plane coordinates `(Z,-A)` and `(Z,+A)`, with `Z` and `A` the actual admitted rational values of source `0.23` and positive source `0.1`. They are not substituted with decimal fractions. The exact coefficient pair is `D=w*w-s*s`, `C=2*w*s`.

The selected schedule retains the left eye's completed products `p=D*Z`, `n=C*(-A)`, `q=C*Z`, and `m=D*(-A)`. Left floors remain `floor128(p-n)` and `floor128(q+m)`. Right floors become `floor128(p+n)` and `floor128(q-m)`, at their original locations. The independent mathematics review establishes the exact rational identities and all four zero/nonzero coefficient branches.

When `D` and `C` are nonzero, the replacement removes four right-eye exact multiplications while keeping its two combines and floors. Its raw arithmetic benefit is at most four operations per paired evaluation visit. The zero-coefficient branches yield three, one or zero operations; they do not increase that ceiling. Literal reuse without the sign transformation would remove only two multiplications.

Sources are `ontokin/reach_reference_center.py:658–686`, the head/eye definitions in `docs/language-design/planar-reach-v0.1/libraries/reach-assets.ok`, and the source branch calculation in `math.md`.

The current evaluator already shares orientation coefficients, topological plans, stationary poses and preceding-endpoint comparison results. Preparation retains a coefficient source only for repeated projections within its existing orientation group (`reach_reference_center.py:408–429`). The original P11 moving-plan order ends with brow, left eye and right eye under the same head orientation. Their coefficient lookups, native-domain checks and cache effects remain in the baseline. They cannot be credited again as C065 savings.

Original L03 has unit offsets at the upper hinge tip and tool. Its source authorities are different shoulder and forearm orientations, and `product(value,1)` already returns `value` without multiplication. Neither its first nor second authored action supplies a corresponding nontrivial repeated offset product. Equal lengths do not establish equal orientations or common source authority.

## Concrete charged-copy schedule

The design selected for this review has a source-only pair descriptor and one transient four-value bridge within a single `evaluate` call. Each left product is detached only after its original arithmetic operation completes. Right evaluation retains its ordinary node, recipe, parent and coefficient admission. It then validates and consumes the bridge. All temporary state is discarded on every rejection and before the next key.

The following ledger deliberately gives the design favorable bounds. Work that is not needed to establish rejection is left nonnegative, rather than assigned an invented exact count.

| Added work | Bound for the selected schedule | Reason |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Compile and admit the source pair descriptor | At least zero over the request | Existing preparation is unchanged; every new inspected vector, relation, grouping lookup and descriptor must be funded. |
| Detach/admit `p` after its original production | One charge per completed bridge | One newly copied logical scalar occurrence. |
| Detach/admit `n` after its original production | One charge per completed bridge | A distinct product occurrence, even if an exceptional numerical value equals another result. |
| Detach/admit `q` after its original production | One charge per completed bridge | One newly copied logical scalar occurrence. |
| Detach/admit `m` after its original production | One charge per completed bridge | One newly copied logical scalar occurrence. |
| Publish/store and select/load the bridge | At least zero beyond those four charges | No existing operation is identified as paying for this new bridge. Actual publication and selection cannot simply be omitted from the final ledger. |
| New type, leaf, bound, seal, source-claim and native-dispatch checks | At least zero beyond those four charges | The existing recipe/parent checks are retained and do not automatically authenticate a new arithmetic receipt. |
| Detached consumer copies, if used | Four further scalar charges per full consumption | The selected per-scalar tariff also applies to new copied return values. |
| Rejected selections, partial bridges, cleanup and fallback | At least zero | Failed attempts do not earn the right-eye arithmetic saving. |

The one-charge-per-scalar materialization/admission rule is an explicit property of this conservative candidate. It is not an assertion that all Python assignments inherently charge the current runtime. In particular, existing `_Guard.freeze` constructs a detached `Fraction` and guards its fields without its own tick. Calling it four additional times and declaring that new work free would leave the C065 accounting requirement unmet. A different bounded, funded bulk operation would be a different candidate needing an explicit authority and accounting proof; none is assumed here.

Let `N` be the number of completed bridges over every attempt in the complete request, including any replay. Let `g(e)` be the corresponding raw arithmetic benefit, with `g(e) <= 4`. With original coefficient/cache/cursor work preserved, the selected schedule has

`net saving <= sum(g(e)) - 4*N - C_other <= -C_other <= 0`.

`C_other` includes all remaining added preparation, admission, storage, selection, consumer-copy, partial-attempt, cleanup and fallback work. A bridge that is partly built or never consumed adds work but removes no right-eye operations. Degenerate coefficients and stationary/unsupported paths only reduce its raw opportunity. Consequently neither an exact future visit count nor a guessed tail cost is needed to reject this schedule's strictly positive premise. Unproven improvements to another cache, callback route or retry path cannot be inserted as a negative overhead credit.

## Existing-coefficient-cache variant

Storing the four products inside the existing coefficient entry would avoid creating a second cross-key cache, but it does not fund the selected detached-copy schedule.

`_coefficients` performs its lookup or cold publication before `_offset` computes the four offset products (`reach_reference_center.py:592–655`, then `674–682`). Therefore the left coefficient operation cannot already have published values that did not yet exist. Attaching the products requires a later publication/update with its own admitted record and accounting. The four producer materializations remain. Retaining detached consumer return values adds another four materializations.

The right coefficient lookup already executes in the baseline. A design may use its result to locate an extended receipt, but may not claim that unchanged lookup as a newly removed charge or assume it pays for arbitrary extra source, dispatch, leaf and copy checks. `_Arithmetic._sync_cache_operand_limit` states that an existing cache charge covers its usual bounded bookkeeping; extending a receipt needs its own explicit work proof.

An actual cache extension would also need to specify whether products die after the right-eye use or remain attached until replacement; how rejection clears them; and how live operand-cap changes, cache replacement and eviction preserve source and cursor authority. Replacing the existing same-source entry is not automatically an extra cache slot, but an unchanged entry count alone does not prove unchanged lifetime, admission or future cache cost. This review does not assign beneficial cache effects without that proof. The detached-copy schedule has already exhausted its arithmetic opportunity before these questions could provide a valid positive premise.

## Full original requests, tails and replay

The retained request options remain `fps=1.0`, `helpers=false`, P11 end `3`, L03 end `4`, and their original tolerances. The authored P11 actions end at time 2; the L03 actions run over `[0,2]` and `[2,3]`. No later attachment or root-motion event creates another source pair for this candidate.

The complete supplied clock arrays are retained even though the independent reader failed. Read-only inspection of the verified C061 archive and the two identified GLB members confirms:

- P11 has 52 keys: `0, 1/25, ..., 49/25, 2, 3`.
- L03 has 178 keys, with its final keys ending `264/89, 265/89, 266/89, 3, 4`.

These GLBs are retained unvalidated reader inputs. Their presence does not establish successful source validation or a delivered artifact. Nor do their key counts establish that every key enters the eligible centered path.

`_key_comparisons` has at most one optional centered-reader call per invocation, and a single `compare_certificates` traversal calls it once per supplied key (`reach_conformance.py:1997–2002`, `3388–3401`). Thus one unchanged P11 traversal has the generous gross ceiling `4*52 = 208`, before all new work. Full plans can fail centered preparation because their leg/column mounts have nonidentity local rotations. Stationary reuse, zero coefficients, missing pairs and completed phases can reduce the opportunity. No successful unvisited tail is inferred.

The surrounding certificate loop can replay on the same work allowance (`reach_conformance.py:3449` onward). Therefore **208 is a single-traversal ceiling, not a universal whole-request bound under fallback or replay**. The per-bridge rejection above includes every attempt and does not require a no-replay assumption. A different surviving design would need a complete account of all replay, phase, reseed, rejected-selection, final-admission and cleanup effects.

The last row is a conservative partial-tail lower bound, not the whole tail. It counts unchanged key/row gates and omits other work. Under the additional assumption of the same single traversal, unchanged producer, gates and cache/retry pattern, even P11's overly favorable 208 gross arithmetic ceiling is smaller than that selected mandatory future work. That is a conditional inability to finish the original request, separate from the development-premise rejection. A small fully funded positive increment could be worthwhile without itself completing a request; this selected charged-copy increment does not establish even that smaller result.

No phase-construction, geometry, coefficient, orientation-cursor, reseed, independent-comparison, row-admission or finalization saving is credited to this offset-only candidate. All original limits remain: 100,000 shared operations, 30,000 ms, 32,768 operand bits, 256 numerical-cache entries and 64 cursor updates.
