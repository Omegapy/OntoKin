## 2. The finite authority and charging graph

A list of conditions is not an executable safe gate. The proposed check must itself inspect, charge and retain values without invoking unadmitted behavior or relying on a fact invalidated by its last callback. C054 separates these families:

| Family | Source obligation |
| --- | --- |
| Inspection and precharge | Establish trusted reads of class, function and raw storage, plus a valid charge path for every new predicate/read/copy. Expected code/state references need their own provenance. |
| Parent arithmetic | Current arithmetic owner, bits/scale, live and cached operand domain, cache identity, check owner and both arithmetic observer slots. |
| Child value access | Safely inspect the current child cache and exact endpoint representation. Exact-value authentication and fresh copies do not inherently require reauthenticating the child's already finished callback history or unused work fields. |
| Shared work | `ReachExecutionWork.tick` and `charge`, their current code/default/global dependencies, external check, counters, limits, elapsed clock, count map, observer and failure path. |
| Construction callback | `ConstructionBudget.tick`, nested `check`, limits/counters/clock and `exceeded`. A native outer bound method does not make the nested check absent. |
| Numerical counterfactual | Current helpers and Fraction/Interval constructor/operator/descriptor dependencies needed to establish what the omitted native pi computation would return and which original guards could refuse. |
| Certificate and copies | Exact independently derived value, fixed code/storage provenance, normalized integer representation, fresh rational/interval construction and every logical retained occurrence. |
| Publication | Native `remember` tick, operand-domain synchronization and then prepaid final admission. Actual occupancy and key conditions must be current after these effects. |
| First consumer | Caller target-map assignment, active-target assignment, source/action/pose owners, descriptors and all intervening cache probes before pi is used. |
| Runtime/lifetime | Current trace/profile and dictionary watcher behavior, concurrent mutation, finalizers, aliases and error/cancellation/cap behavior. |

The actual callback order matters. In `_Arithmetic.tick` (reference lines 110–117), both arithmetic observers are captured before `self.check()`; product then ordinary observer run after its normal return. In `ReachExecutionWork.charge` (numeric lines 71–94), the work observer is captured before `external_check`, while its invocation follows the original resource and counter checks. `ConstructionBudget.tick` (construction lines 75–88) invokes its nested check before counting and checking elapsed time. Original callback exceptions retain their identity.

Checking an observer slot later does not suppress a callback already captured by an active tick. The final callback-free admission must follow all relevant precharge, publication-tick and synchronization effects. The work count map and construction limit map also have key/value dispatch; exact dictionary type alone does not close their behavior.

There is a concrete interface issue. `_canonical_targets(payload, arithmetic)` has no reference, action, active-action or target-map argument, and the arithmetic object has no reference-owner back-pointer. Yet after its return, `_event` reads the parent domain, may assign `self._targets[key]`, assigns `active.targets`, and enters `_base_tip`. A proposed proof of those owners requires an explicitly supplied context or a different boundary. Their authority cannot be inferred from the existing two helper arguments.

## 3. D36: a specific conservative screen already exceeds the room

The frozen D36 strategy applies the same five predicates to each named reachable Python callable: current binding, `__code__`, `__defaults__`, `__kwdefaults__` and `__closure__` reference. Each identity predicate receives its own charge. D36 deliberately includes expected-`None` fields.

The inventory contains:

- **19 arithmetic entries:** operand-limit getter, dyadic denominator, cache-domain synchronization, tick, guard, exact, point, grid, grid-domain, sum-enclosure, interval, sub, corner-domain, mul, remember, lookup, small atan, pi and sincos.
- **Work, construction and interval functions, totaling five entries:** work tick and charge; construction tick; generated Interval initializer and post-initializer.
- **12 Fraction entries:** constructor; addition, subtraction, multiplication and division wrappers; Boolean, equality, less-than, greater-than and greater-or-equal operations; numerator and denominator getters.

The schedule JSON and independent cost review enumerate all **36** names. CPython 3.12.14 Fraction and dataclass source establish the referenced callable forms. The product modules were read as text/AST; no import was used to manufacture a runtime identity claim.

One complete D36 pass therefore has a predicate floor of `36*5=180`. It does not need a second complete pass to fail. The native cold parent pi costs 165, its hit costs one, and the added early remember costs one: `165-1-1=163` remains before every other new cost. Thus D36 has an optimistic local ceiling of **163-180=-17**, or **-18** when the separately required entry discriminator is included.

This is a rejection of **this generic checking strategy**. It is not a claim that every safe gate needs five metadata checks per callable. Most of the arithmetic functions have no defaults; a proof tied to an actual invocation may omit metadata it cannot read. A closure reference can be stable while its cells change, and globals can change without a new function object. Reads, cell contents, transitive Fraction delegates, globals, builtin/class authority, original failure helpers, owner storage, safe precharge, key scans and copies remain additional. D36 is not a complete safe executable gate even before its cost fails.

No claim depends on counting the finished child's unused work/observer fields as mandatory. A different use-sensitive proof is not rejected by D36's chosen overhead.

## 4. T1: authentic values can change after the pi-only screen

T1's literal resident-key screen requires an exact nonempty tuple whose first component is an exact native string unequal to `"pi"`. For the specific pi key, native tuple equality stops at that first differing string, even if tuple hashes collide. The screen can therefore make those particular pi-key comparisons passive without recursively inspecting resident tails.

It does not establish passivity of a different query. The unchanged route is:

`_event -> _base_tip lookup -> sincos lookup -> pi lookup`.

`_PoseArithmetic._key` (reference lines 837–838) makes the first probe:

`("reference_pose",128,32768,"base_tip",lengths,coordinates)`.

A resident tuple with that same tag passes T1. Its later components may execute equality during this probe.

### Conditional source witness

Take a native successful first128 path with ordinary lengths `l`, returned coordinate tuple `c=(first,second)`, `first != 0`, and genuine absent base-tip, sincos and pi entries. These are premises, not measured setup incidence.

Let the sole pre-publication resident key be:

`r=("reference_pose",128,32768,"base_tip",l,E)`.

The stored value can be harmless and native. The custom final component `E` returns `hash(c)` from its hash operation. Its equality method, when compared with `c`, does the following: read the parent pi entry, if present change only that Interval's `lo` and `hi` fields to the native rational `25/8` through `object.__setattr__`, then return exactly `False`. It does nothing when pi is absent. No executable witness or rational computation was run.

Both pi-only key scans admit `r`. Early insertion of pi does not call `E` because the first tuple tags differ. The new interval is authentic at publication, with fresh objects and no child alias.

After the original target return, `_base_tip` creates its real query with final component `c`. Equal tuple length and component hashes give the resident and query the same hash. CPython's generic dictionary lookup compares the stored resident tuple to the query; tuple equality reaches `E.__eq__(c)` after the equal prefix. The candidate already contains pi, so its fresh Interval is changed. The original has not published parent pi, so the same equality call finds nothing to change.

The equality method returns `False`, so both base-tip lookups still genuinely miss. It changes no dictionary key, table, order or membership. No iterator failure or dictionary-restart assumption is needed. The first nonzero sincos then reaches pi: the candidate gets the altered value, while the original calculates its native result.

A frozen dataclass is not an adversarially immutable capability against `object.__setattr__`. Fresh copies solve the child alias issue, but their fields still need protection through use.

The source witness uses **one resident key**, independently of the large-occupancy cost bounds. It requires no active callback, arithmetic observer, trace hook, destructor or method replacement during T1. It defeats the use of the pi-only screen as sufficient first-consumer closure, even granting a correct certificate, safe precharge and current native callable authority. A design that separately authenticates these deeper key components has added the missing obligation and is a different, separately priced gate.

The lookup/hash/equality argument is grounded in CPython 3.12.14 [tuple source](https://github.com/python/cpython/blob/v3.12.14/Objects/tupleobject.c) and [dictionary source](https://github.com/python/cpython/blob/v3.12.14/Objects/dictobject.c), with exact blob identities in the source binding. It does not certify runtime observer absence or supported 3.11/3.13 execution.

## 5. T2 repairs one gap, with additional obligations still open

The literal T2 screen rejects three first tags: `pi`, `reference_pose` and `sincos`. Under native stable query construction and tuple/string comparison, every relevant resident tuple then differs at its first string for all three immediate cache probes. This excludes the demonstrated `E` witness.

T1 performs four per-key predicates per pass: tuple type, nonempty, first-component string type and pi-tag inequality. Its two-pass floor is `4*(m_before+m_after)`, or `8m` with stable occupancy. At stable **m=21**, predicates alone consume 168, exceeding 163; at m=20 they leave at most three.

T2's chosen linear comparison schedule performs those shape checks and three tag inequalities. Its two-pass floor is `6*(m_before+m_after)`, or `12m` with stable occupancy. At stable **m=14**, predicates alone consume 168; at m=13 they leave at most seven. These are sufficient, non-sharp rejection thresholds for the literal screens. A differently organized set-membership test would require its own accounting. The occupancies refer to the two screens before early insertion; actual setup occupancy remains unknown.

T2 is not complete corridor closure. A target-map insertion can call a resident key's equality or retire a replaced value. Assigning `active.targets` can retire its previous value. Source/payload and pose-owner fields can have effectful descriptors. A narrow route might require a native empty target map, an initially `None` active target, passively readable source owners and an explicit caller context; none is a free inherited fact. Shared charge-map safety, precharge bootstrap and runtime observers remain separate.

C054 does not claim that every repaired early-publication design is impossible. It establishes no complete safe executable T2 gate and no positive complete bound. More incidence data would not repair T1 or make D36 cheaper. No collector is selected.

## 6. Costs and failed paths retain their meaning

For matched completed early-publication executions:

`saving = 164*H - Q - A_nonpublication - Delta_remaining`.

H counts realized native cold misses actually replaced by authentic hits. Q includes every reached new publication tick, including final admission rejection. A includes every other reached discriminator, check, read, precharge, certificate access, copied/retained occurrence and cleanup. Delta is the disjoint signed remaining-work difference. It can be zero only under a separately established complete convergence premise; T1 does not establish that premise.

Target-map hits and original canonical failures bypass new work. Later-precision successes pay the new discriminator but do not publish. Missing child pi, unsupported authority, occupied/disallowed parent keys and rejected final admission retain their reached costs without a cold-miss credit. Unused or invalidated publication cannot claim 164. Repeated targets do not create repeated first-parent-miss savings.

Interrupted callbacks and operation/elapsed/operand refusals require exact prefix and exception-identity reasoning. A completed-cost identity cannot invent successful work after the original cap. H, Q, actual occupancy, complete gate cost and complete-request net remain unmeasured. Subtracting a theoretical local opportunity from an observed failed 100001 count would not establish a passing recording.

Numerical constant generation remains a separately declared and independently reviewed proof task. The certificate must equal the original native result, not merely enclose mathematical pi, copy a host constant or trust producer output.
