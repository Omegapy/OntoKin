# C053 independent child-pi publication authority review

Reviewer: `/root/authority_review`. Source parent: C052 `853c01589af790bc83bf52ed70b18dc87673873b`, tree `cadf049f90b45dbcf234199acb2e25a3f4c0eec8`. Scope: [Issue26 comment 6451681679](https://github.com/Omegapy/OntoKin/issues/26#issuecomment-6451681679). The selected inputs and inspection limits are in [input-binding.json](input-binding.json).

**Decision: S1 is NO-GO.** Its new admission begins only after the original first128 canonical calculation succeeds. Current native structure, current callback absence, and current cache identity do not establish that the child's resident pi value is the result that the original independent factory completed. A callback can replace only that cache mapping at the final original rounding tick, retire itself, and leave both the original target and the later structural screen unchanged. S1 then transfers the replacement to the parent. The final publication admission closes a stale-absence race, but does not repair this earlier history gap.

This finding rejects the specified schedule. It does not reject every possible transfer, independently proved value certificate, or protected-lifetime design. No OntoKin module, numerical candidate, original request, runtime test, benchmark, archived driver, or hosted job was executed for this review. Source reading and file hash metadata are the only executed activities. No observed request corruption, exact hostile-request result, or measured saving is claimed.

## 1. Selected schedule and proof boundary

S1 retains the original `_canonical_targets` common algebra, fresh child construction at every precision, atan operations, interval calculations, first `_Uncertain` refinement, both `round64` calls, half-turn normalization, and closed joint limits. Its optional path starts after all those original success checks, immediately before the original successful return.

The [frozen coordinator schedule](../../checkpoint-053-schedule.json), with its [all-path ledger](../../checkpoint-053-cost-ledger.json), has this order:

1. Charge optional admission entry and discriminate first128 success. Unsupported or later-precision cases retain the original return after the new work actually reached.
2. Inspect current owner, method/code/global, domain, observer and native-container state. This is a present-state screen. It retains no record of child construction or intervening mutations and independently authenticates no expected numerical endpoint value.
3. Require exactly one matching child pi entry with native `Interval` and `Fraction` endpoint structure. Empty, malformed or unsupported child states fall back.
4. Scan every resident parent key for recursively passive native key components, then make a genuinely nonpromoting absence check. A present key causes fallback without consuming its payload or changing its order.
5. Select the existing child `Interval` by alias. Do not compute missing pi and do not rebuild its endpoint values.
6. Use the native parent `remember` operation, including its original tick and operand-domain synchronization. Its final, callback-free admission rechecks the current domain, child/payload identity, and actual parent absence after that tick and synchronization. If those conditions fail, do not publish.
7. All added inspections, copied or retained occurrences, scans, and rechecks are charged. At-capacity eviction and its lifetime effects need a separate native-victim proof; that proof is not supplied by key safety alone.

The coordinator tariff requires a shared precharge before each newly reached logical check, lookup, copied or retained occurrence. Its callback-free final admission must be prepaid, then reread current state after the publication tick. This is a rejected declarative schedule: its exact safe executable precharge graph and complete charge total are unclosed. The authority rejection below does not assign any of these checks zero cost or rely on an unclosed precharge path. It remains valid even if every current-state check is implemented correctly and all new callback boundaries are safe after the original successful calculation.

## 2. What the original child establishes under an unchanged native lifetime

[`reach_reference.py`](../../../../../ontokin/reach_reference.py) lines 674–735 define the canonical calculation. At line 710 a new `_Arithmetic` uses the existing work/check authority and the chosen precision. Its constructor creates an empty dictionary, fixes its scale from the live operand domain, and sets both arithmetic observer slots to `None` (lines 72–87). Construction has no numerical tick, but its original denominator refusal remains meaningful.

The precision body reaches the cache only through `pi`; it does not call `sincos`, the factorial factory, or pose caching. Under the unchanged native constructor, method graph, rational arithmetic and callback graph, the cache therefore has zero or one pi entry. Its first pi call misses, computes the original independent Machin enclosure, and remembers that completed result (lines 389–395). Later child pi calls reuse that result.

This gives a conditional origin theorem: if the entire construction-to-publication interval excludes unsupported writers and dispatch changes, the one resident matching pi value is the completed original result. The theorem is about that interval, not about the type of the cache at its end. A fresh empty child is insufficient once an intervening callback can reach its mutable dictionary.

Native exact endpoints also matter. A merely valid enclosure of mathematical pi need not equal the original enclosure and can change subsequent interval width, reduction, or rounding behavior. The transfer must establish the exact values required by the independent algorithm and the live operand/prospective domain. Positivity, ordering, dyadic denominators, and a broad `3 < pi < 4` screen do not establish that result.

## 3. Replace-and-retire history witness against S1

Consider a first128 call that reaches both original target roundings successfully, has constructed child pi, and has a genuinely absent matching parent pi key. All ordinary numerical methods, source values, limits and parent state are native. The already installed callback graph is:

```text
child arithmetic tick
  -> native ReachExecutionWork.tick/charge
  -> native ConstructionBudget.tick
  -> its preinstalled nested check callback
```

This is a supported shape in the unmodified source. [`reach_numeric.py`](../../../../../ontokin/reach_numeric.py) lines 71–97 invoke `external_check` before work counter updates. [`construction.py`](../../../../../ontokin/construction.py) lines 76–88 invoke the budget's nested `check`, preserve an exception object if it raises, and otherwise continue normally. No original canonical gate excludes that nested callback.

Let the nested callback remain inert until the tick in the second and final `ar.round64(elbow)` call. The source already computed the shoulder and elbow `Interval` objects before either rounding (reference lines 714–727). At this tick, `round64` holds its current `value` argument and has not yet converted its endpoints (lines 638–652).

The callback performs these actions and returns normally:

1. Obtain the current child arithmetic object from the active `round64` Python frame. Ordinary frame introspection such as `sys._getframe` does not require installing a trace or profile hook. This describes one possible callback mechanism; it was not executed here.
2. Replace only `ar.cache[("pi", 128, 32768)]` with a fresh native `Interval(F(25, 8), F(25, 8))`.
3. Set its owning `ConstructionBudget.check` to `None`, retiring itself before S1 begins.

The callback leaves the source payload, parent cache, arithmetic/work owners, method/code/global identities, arithmetic observer slots, and the already computed target intervals untouched. It does not mutate the previous pi `Interval`; it replaces a dictionary mapping. Even if an elbow argument aliases that previous genuine interval, the argument still refers to the old genuine object. The callback's own executing closure keeps it alive until return, and no callback error is recorded because it raises nothing.

The original `round64` call therefore converts the same elbow endpoints. Half-turn normalization and closed-limit checks use the same values and payload. Original success or failure at those checks is unchanged. On the successful path under review, the candidate and original obtain the same canonical target tuple.

At the start of S1, however, the child cache now has exactly one native matching pi entry with positive, ordered, dyadic endpoints equal to `25/8`. The nested callback is absent. The relevant current native functions and owner fields have not changed. A present-state structural screen has no record of the replacement. Selecting that entry and rechecking the selected object's identity later proves only that the replacement stayed the same during S1.

The parent `remember` final admission also passes in this witness: there are no subsequent mutation callbacks, the parent key is still absent, and the selected child mapping remains the same native object. It publishes the false entry. On the unchanged original path, the temporary child and its replaced cache are discarded at return; the subsequent cold parent pi call performs the independent factory and cannot obtain `25/8` as its authentic result. The exact C052 series bound already separates the original enclosure from `25/8` by more than 0.015 before the small grid allowance.

This is a source-level distinction in the value supplied at the later parent pi use. It does not require asserting a specific unexecuted residual, exact first128 request incidence, or a newly passed/failed recording. The original canonical target is preserved while the added transfer introduces invalid independent numerical authority. That directly violates the transfer's required origin premise.

The witness does not defeat a gate that closes the callback/dispatch graph before child construction and proves it stays closed, or a gate that independently verifies the exact numerical endpoint value after all possible mutation. S1 has neither mechanism.

## 4. Current authority, charging, and publication are separate obligations

S1 deliberately improves on bare `if key not in parent.cache: parent.remember(...)`. Native `remember` at reference lines 346–356 first ticks, then synchronizes the live operand limit, then runs its optional admission before changing cache contents. A callback in that tick can otherwise insert the previously absent key. Without final admission, `remember` deletes the now-present key and overwrites it. S1's fresh absence/identity/domain recheck after the tick prevents this particular race, assuming that recheck itself is safe and non-effectful.

That repair does not authenticate what happened before S1. Likewise, a currently absent observer does not prove that an observer was absent during child arithmetic. A currently original function/code/default/closure/global state does not prove that every earlier call used it. Current native authority and historical numerical origin must remain distinct in the result record.

A viable gate also needs a safe charging route. Newly added checks must use the same live work and caps, without invoking an unproved custom method or swallowing its failure. The original callback exception identity, operand/prospective refusals, and first-refinement semantics remain required. No current type test should be treated as an uncharged trusted fact simply to avoid that issue. The exact gate must cover class storage and descriptors before reading potentially effectful fields; function identity alone does not cover a modified `__code__`, default, closure cell, module global, or rational implementation.

The centered `_Guard.original` in [`reach_reference_center.py`](../../../../../ontokin/reach_reference_center.py) lines 276–309 is a real observer used by later centered operations. It preserves narrowed-domain original failures and cannot be silently ignored. Ordinary native canonical targets are built earlier during `ReachReference` construction, so that centered observer is not assumed active in the ordinary constructor corridor. This timing fact narrows the needed graph; it does not close the nested work/construction callback demonstrated above.

## 5. Safe keys, capacity, and retained aliases

An exact native `dict` does not make all of its resident keys passive. A new membership probe may call a resident key's equality method after a hash collision. S1's scan is therefore a substantive safety operation with real cost. It must occur under a proved no-mutation interval or explicitly account for mutations during its charged checks. Ordinary parent `lookup` is unsuitable as an order-neutral rejection probe because it promotes present values by deleting and reinserting them (reference lines 358–368).

S1 chooses a recursive passive-key scan. That is a conservative schedule choice, not a universal minimum. For a specific pi lookup, an exact native tuple whose first element is an exact native string unequal to `"pi"` can reject equality before examining unrelated tail elements. A more specialized proof could exploit this, but it would be a different gate and require its own cost and mutation analysis. At capacity, eviction also hashes the selected victim key again, so the narrow absence proof alone is not enough for that mutation.

Key safety does not establish value lifetime safety. Early eviction can release an arbitrary victim value; if that value has effectful destruction, it can execute code even when the numerical key probe was passive. A no-eviction gate or a specific native victim/value-origin proof is needed. The existing 256-entry limit is unchanged, and no additional persistent cache or hidden allowance is allowed.

Aliasing the child's `Interval` avoids endpoint reconstruction but still creates a new retained cache occurrence that must be priced. `Interval` is a frozen dataclass, and native `Fraction` values are used immutably by the ordinary arithmetic. That interface does not itself prove there is no leaked reference, object-level mutation, custom descriptor, or identity observer. A protected native lifetime can justify the alias; present frozen/native type names alone cannot.

## 6. Conditional cache convergence and its exact limits

The source offers a useful positive conditional result. After `_canonical_targets` returns, native `_event` lines 3495–3501 saves the target and calls `_base_tip`. Native `_base_tip` lines 3730–3737 checks its own cache and calls the first `sincos`. Native `sincos` lines 612–621 checks its own cache and, for a nonzero uncached value, calls parent `pi`.

Suppose all of the following hold: the child result is authentic; the parent pi key is absent; precision and operand domain stay fixed; the intervening event/target-map/owner operations are passive; base-tip and the first nonzero sine/cosine lookup both miss; there is no admitted callback or observer that can mutate or distinguish the altered history; and no unrelated parent cache operation occurs before that pi call.

Let the parent ordered cache before the transfer be `K`. Early publication appends pi to `K`, evicting the oldest entry if required by the same fixed capacity. The original later cold pi computation does not touch other parent cache families before its own `remember`, so that later publication produces the same ordered key sequence. The candidate's pi hit promotes a key already at the end. Consequently the two caches can agree in ordered keys and numerical value contents at the first later pi return, even at capacity.

This is not literal object identity. Original parent pi construction creates its own `Interval`/`Fraction` objects; the candidate retained child objects. Under no child reference escape, no identity-sensitive observers, and native consumers, the retained alias graphs can be equivalent up to object renaming. A claim of zero changed later work must use that restricted equivalence and preserve all caps, successful completion assumptions, and subsequent cache behavior. Work counters themselves differ by the actual new and omitted work.

The theorem does not cover a base-tip or sine/cosine hit, a zero first angle, domain reset, reentrant callback, malformed target key, custom owner assignment, early cancellation, or any later consumer that observes identity or a leaked child alias. Those paths need their own ledger and behavior review. In particular, native `_targets` insertion and `active.targets` assignment are passive only under their own owner/key assumptions; they are not universally free of user code.

The cost reviewer separately establishes that a successful original native128 parent pi miss costs 165 and a hit costs 1. C053 keeps all child canonical work. Its conditional opportunity is one later 164-operation miss-to-hit replacement, before new admission/publication costs. This authority review does not turn that opportunity into a measured request saving.

## 7. Distinct next hypothesis: exact-value certification

A concrete next source question can remove S1's dependence on cache history: independently derive and authenticate the exact original native128 pi endpoint values, then require the selected child's current endpoint integer fields to equal that certificate in a safe final admission. This is a nomination, not a selected implementation or completed proof. It does not waive safe precharge/current callback closure, parent keys, alias lifetime, prospective original limits, or a positive complete cost bound.

The original factory gives a finite derivation target. At bits128, the threshold is `2^-136`. For `v=1/5`, the first accepted series stop is the 28-term sum through index27; for `v=1/239`, it is the eight-term sum through index7. Those first-stop facts have the source inequalities retained in C052 and must be independently rebound in a new certificate. Let `A_lo/A_hi` be the exact outward-grid endpoints of the first sum and its signed following-term bound; let `B_lo/B_hi` be the corresponding second endpoints. The subsequent native arithmetic gives exact endpoint expressions `16*A_lo - 4*B_hi` and `16*A_hi - 4*B_lo`, on the original dyadic grid.

A certificate must show those values arise from the original first strict stop, endpoint grid, multiplication, and subtraction rules, including native rational normalization and every prospective operand bound. Hard-coded host `math.pi`, decimal approximations, producer values, a generic pi-containing interval, or an unreviewed table are not replacements for that derivation. The actual certificate integers, their immutable source binding, the exact comparison schedule, and the complete cost remain unproduced here.

This direction can reject the replace-and-retire value even when its past writer is unknown, because exact current endpoint equality is a numerical proof rather than an origin inference. It still has to exclude mutation between inspection and retained use. If the fixed certificate plus safe current gate is too expensive or cannot preserve original errors, that exact design must be rejected with its own bound. No collector or fresh original request is selected by this nomination.
